\paragraph{Quantifier and Equality Proof Patterns.}

Predicate logic supplies formal deduction rules. In ordinary mathematical
writing, those rules appear as repeatable proof moves. The table below records
the translation from formal rule to human proof habit.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.28\textwidth} p{0.30\textwidth} p{0.32\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Goal or Given Form} & \textbf{Formal Rule Behind It} & \textbf{Proof Move} \\
\midrule
Prove $\forall x\,P(x)$ &
  Universal generalization &
  Fix an arbitrary $x$ and prove $P(x)$. \\

Use $\forall x\,P(x)$ &
  Universal instantiation &
  Substitute the particular object or term you need. \\

Prove $\exists x\,P(x)$ &
  Existential generalization &
  Construct a witness and verify the property. \\

Use $\exists x\,P(x)$ &
  Existential instantiation &
  Name a fresh witness and carry its defining property forward. \\

Prove $\exists!x\,P(x)$ &
  Existence plus equality comparison &
  Produce one witness, then assume two witnesses and prove they are equal. \\

Use $a=b$ &
  Equality elimination &
  Replace one term by the other inside a legitimate formula. \\

Prove term equality $s=t$ &
  Reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, substitution &
  Rewrite, chain equalities, or show both terms reduce to the same expression. \\

Prove set equality $A=B$ &
  Extensionality &
  Prove $A\subseteq B$ and $B\subseteq A$, or prove equivalent membership tests. \\

Show a definition is well-defined &
  Equality-respecting construction &
  Check that equal inputs or equivalent representatives give equal outputs. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}[Formal rule versus proof technique]
The rules themselves belong to predicate logic and the identity/equality
foundation. The proof technique is the habit they create in written
mathematics: fix an arbitrary object, produce a witness, substitute equals,
compare two witnesses, or check that a construction ignores accidental names.
\end{remark}
