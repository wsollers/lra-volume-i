\begin{theorem*}[Symmetry of Equality]
If \(x=y\), then \(y=x\).
\end{theorem*}

\begin{proof}[Professional Standard Proof]
\LRAProofBodyStart
Assume \(x=y\). Let \(\Phi(z)\) be the formula \(z=x\). By reflexivity of
equality, \(x=x\), so \(\Phi(x)\) holds. Since \(x=y\), substitution of
identicals applied to the formula-context \(\Phi\) gives \(\Phi(y)\). Hence
\(y=x\). Therefore \(x=y\to y=x\), and universal generalization gives
\[
  \forall x\,\forall y\,(x=y\to y=x).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Detailed Learning Proof]
\LRAProofBodyStart
We want to prove that equality can be reversed. So suppose first that
\[
  x=y.
\]
The substitution axiom cannot be used in isolation; it must be applied to a
formula that is already known to hold. The known formula comes from
reflexivity:
\[
  x=x.
\]

Now choose the formula-context
\[
  \Phi(z) \quad\text{to mean}\quad z=x.
\]
In this context, the variable \(z\) marks the single position where
substitution is being performed, while the displayed \(x\) on the right side
is held fixed as a parameter. With this choice,
\[
  \Phi(x) \quad\text{is}\quad x=x,
\]
which is true by reflexivity. Since the assumption says \(x=y\), substitution
of identicals transfers the truth of \(\Phi(x)\) to \(\Phi(y)\). But
\[
  \Phi(y) \quad\text{is}\quad y=x.
\]
Thus \(y=x\). Since this was derived from the arbitrary assumption \(x=y\), we
have proved \(x=y\to y=x\), and hence
\[
  \forall x\,\forall y\,(x=y\to y=x).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}[Proof structure]
The key move is to use reflexivity first and substitution second. Reflexivity
supplies the true statement \(x=x\). Substitution then changes the selected
first occurrence of \(x\) into \(y\) inside the formula-context \(z=x\), while
the right-hand \(x\) remains fixed.
\end{remark*}
