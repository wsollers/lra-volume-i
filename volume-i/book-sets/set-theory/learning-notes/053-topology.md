# Gate 53: Topology

## Target

Introduce `def:topology`.

## Statement Shape

Let \(X\) be a set.  A topology on \(X\) is a family
\(\tau\subseteq\mathcal P(X)\) such that:

1. \(\varnothing\in\tau\);
2. \(X\in\tau\);
3. \(\tau\) is closed under arbitrary unions;
4. \(\tau\) is closed under finite intersections.

The members of \(\tau\) are called open sets.

## Why This Gate Exists

Borel sigma-algebras are generated from open sets.  Before defining Borel
sets, the chapter needs a lightweight set-family definition of topology.

The contrast is the main learning point:

- topologies have arbitrary union closure and finite intersection closure;
- sigma-algebras have complement closure and countable union closure;
- Borel generation starts from topology and then applies sigma-generation.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating arbitrary unions in a topology as countable unions only.
- Assuming topologies are closed under complements.  That is generally false.
- Forgetting that Borel sets are not merely open sets; they are members of the
  sigma-algebra generated by the open sets.
