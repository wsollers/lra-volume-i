## Statement

Given a closure system `S` and a generator `E`, define:

```text
<E>_S = intersection {A in S | E subset A}
```

Then generation satisfies:

```text
E subset <E>_S
E subset F -> <E>_S subset <F>_S
<E>_S in S
<<E>_S>_S = <E>_S
```

## Dependencies

- `def:collection-system`: collections of collections of subsets.
- `def:closure-system`: closed under arbitrary intersections.
- `def:generated-collection`: intersection of all closed collections
  containing the generator.
- `def:subset`: containment of generators and generated collections.
- `def:indexed-intersection`: the mechanism behind "intersection of all."

## Plain-Language Reading

Generated structures are minimal closed structures containing chosen data.  A
topology generated by a basis, a sigma-algebra generated by a collection, and
the Borel sigma-algebra generated by open sets all use this same pattern.

## Proof Skeleton

1. Expand `<E>_S` as the intersection of every closed collection containing
   `E`.
2. Extensiveness: each eligible closed collection contains `E`, so every
   element of `E` belongs to their intersection.
3. Monotonicity: if `E subset F`, then every closed collection containing `F`
   also contains `E`; compare the indexing classes of closed collections.
4. Closedness: because `S` is a closure system, the intersection of eligible
   members of `S` is again in `S`.
5. Idempotence: once `<E>_S` is already closed and contains itself, generating
   from it gives nothing new.

## Common Failure Modes

- Collapsing a collection into its union.
- Forgetting that a closure system lives in `P(P(X))`.
- Reversing the monotonicity argument.
- Claiming idempotence before proving the generated collection is closed.
