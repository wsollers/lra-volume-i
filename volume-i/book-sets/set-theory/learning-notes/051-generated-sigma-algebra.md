# Gate 51: Generated Sigma-Algebra

## Target

Introduce `def:generated-sigma-algebra`.

## Statement Shape

Let \(\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal P(X)\).  The sigma-algebra generated by
\(\mathcal E\) is

\[
  \sigma(\mathcal E)
  =
  \bigcap\{\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal P(X):
    \Sigma\text{ is a sigma-algebra on }X
    \text{ and }\mathcal E\subseteq\Sigma\}.
\]

## Why This Gate Exists

This is the construction that later produces Borel sigma-algebras and
measurable spaces from a smaller, understandable starting family.

The mental model is:

- choose the sets you want to force into the measurable world;
- look at every sigma-algebra that already contains them;
- intersect all those sigma-algebras;
- the result is the smallest sigma-algebra containing the chosen sets.

## Common Mistakes

- Thinking generation lists all elements explicitly.  Usually it does not; it
  characterizes the smallest closed structure by intersection.
- Forgetting the containment condition \(\mathcal E\subseteq\Sigma\) in the
  candidate family.
- Treating generated sigma-algebras as cardinality objects.  For this chapter,
  the point is closure control, not cardinal arithmetic.
