\begin{corollary*}[Existence and Uniqueness of Symmetric Difference]
For any sets $A$ and $B$, the set
\[
  A\triangle B:=(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)
\]
exists and is unique.
\end{corollary*}

\begin{proof}[Professional Standard Proof]
\LRAProofBodyStart
By the set-difference corollary, \(A\setminus B\) and \(B\setminus A\) exist
and are unique. By the binary-union corollary, their union exists and is
unique. This union is, by definition, \(A\triangle B\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Detailed Learning Proof]
\LRAProofBodyStart
\textbf{Step 1.} Form \(A\setminus B\), the part of \(A\) outside \(B\), and
\(B\setminus A\), the part of \(B\) outside \(A\). Each exists by the
set-difference corollary.

\textbf{Step 2.} Take the binary union of those two already licensed sets.

\textbf{Step 3.} The result is unique because each input construction is unique
and binary union has a unique output.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark*}[Proof structure]
Symmetric difference is a composite construction: two differences followed by
one binary union.
\end{remark*}
