ZFC Model-Theory Card — Five Candidate Forms

Each version separates the native first-order interface from the set-building tools unlocked later by axioms, definitions, order, lattice, relations, and functions.

Approach 1 · Strict Native Language

The Bare ℒ-Structure of ZFC

Signature ℒ∈

Language
ℒ∈ = { ∈ }first-order logic with equality, plus one non-logical binary relation symbol
Relation Symbols
∈ : 2membership is the only primitive mathematical vocabulary
Function Symbols
nonethere are no native operations: no union, intersection, power set, complement, pair, ordered pair, image, or preimage symbols
Constant Symbols
noneeven ∅ is not primitive in the strict language; it is uniquely defined by an axiom

Structure 𝔐

Form
𝔐 = ⟨ M, E ⟩M is a nonempty domain; E ⊆ M × M interprets membership
Satisfaction
𝔐 ⊨ ZFCthe axioms are sentences about E; the structure is just the domain plus its membership relation
Best use: this is the mathematically clean bedrock card. It protects the distinction between primitive language and everything constructed from it.

Approach 2 · Definitional Expansion

Form

Guardrail
This is notation management, not a new set-theoretic operation.
Best use: this is friendlier for readers because it matches how proofs are written, while still marking ∅ as definitional rather than operational.
